`GlassModalBlurTarget` falls back to the complete solid skin when Android refuses its plane. Placed anywhere in the main window (around page content, or in a react-native-screens or expo-router modal screen, which shares the main window on Android), the bridge handed its surfaces the capture plane they sit inside, which `@nannier/canvas-blur` can never sample: the surfaces resolved frost and painted a tint with no blur. The Android glass host now forwards the frost's new `onTargetRejected` report (canvas-blur 0.2) to the nearest bridge, which stops publishing that plane, so every surface and pane host under it resolves its solid skin through the shared material resolution and releases its capture demand (the owner's GMBT-2 decision). The verdict is per bridge and per plane and starts trusting, so a bridge inside a React Native Modal, which never contains the plane, frosts from its first frame as before; with an older canvas-blur nothing changes. Its JSDoc now states the contract: what it provides, that it renders no view, the Android preconditions, where it belongs, what it samples and the no-op platforms. `test/glass-modal-blur-target.test.tsx` holds the verdict and the ActionSheet and Drawer paths through the bridge.
