On Android a pressed control shows its ripple again where its glass material renders. A ripple is the Pressable's background, which Android draws before the view's children, and the GlassPane that paints a control's surface under glass is one of those children: wherever its frost rendered (an overlay outlet, a bridged Modal, the Material 3 bottom sheet a Popover becomes on a phone), it covered the press feedback, and a borderless ripple, projected onto an ancestor's background, landed beneath it too. The press now draws in the foreground wherever the pane renders, over the material and the label in the ripple's own translucent colour, as Compose draws Material 3's state layer: a Button, a tappable Chip, the Select trigger, a pressable Card, MediaObject and Stats card, the current Pagination page, a Radio and its card, the Checkbox box, the Stepper's buttons, the selected Calendar day, the Carousel arrows, the active Navbar link, Sidebar row and TabBar tab, the selected Tabs pill and rail item, a pressable Steps disc, a pressable Avatar tile and ButtonGroup's spaced peers, as well as an Alert's dismiss button and an Input's clear and reveal buttons, whose borderless ripples sat beneath their host's pane. Solid mode, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast and Android's in-page glass, which resolves solid, keep the ripple exactly as before; iOS and the web have no ripple.
