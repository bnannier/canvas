A tap inside one of a component's controls no longer presses the control beside it on iOS and Android. React Native gives a point that two touch areas both cover to the later control, so a touch slop that reached into its neighbor's box took the taps aimed at it: on both platforms a tap on an Input's clear glyph could reveal the password, and on iOS a tap at the end of the value could clear it, a tap on the right edge of a tappable Chip's label removed the chip, a tap on the right edge of a Board card's drag handle opened the card's menu, and a tap just under a vertical Steps circle, over its connector, pressed nothing. Where a component places two of its own controls side by side or stacked, it now splits the gap between their touch areas, from its own padding and sizes, so they meet without overlapping: the Toast's action and dismiss, the Stepper's buttons and its value, a tappable Chip's label and its remove glyph, an Input's clear and eye glyphs and its value, a Board card's drag handle and menu trigger, and the Steps circles down a vertical rail (across a horizontal connector the margins already keep them apart). The Steps connectors and a horizontal step's label take no touches, so a tap in a circle's touch area over either presses the circle. A removable Chip's remove glyph keeps its touch area biased away from the label in a right-to-left layout too. Each touch area keeps the platform minimum on every side that faces no other control; nothing changes size or moves.
