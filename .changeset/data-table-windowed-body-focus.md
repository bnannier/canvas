A `virtualized` DataTable's body is now a keyboard stop the table manages, on the web, in every browser. Its rows scroll in a list of their own under the fixed header, and the table never said whether that list was a tab stop: Chromium and Firefox made it one when nothing inside it could take focus (a read-only table's rows), with the browser's own ring drawn on the list (blue in Chromium, black in Firefox, and on a phone-width table clipped to its top edge), while WebKit left it out of the tab order, so Safari's keyboard could not reach the rows below the fold. The body is now a tab stop while its rows overflow and none while they fit, after the column scroller when a narrow table pans, and while it has keyboard focus the table's surface draws the theme's focus ring for it, just inside the edge of an `attached` table, as it does for the column scroller. As with the column scroller, the rule holds whatever the rows hold, so a windowed table whose rows have controls (selection boxes, row actions) gains the body as one more stop, just before its first row's controls, and the keyboard can always scroll it. The body is the table's row group, so the rows it renders stay inside the table for a screen reader rather than behind an unnamed focusable node. A click into the rows draws no ring until a key is pressed, and a table that switches to its loading rows while the body has focus drops the ring with it.
