A `virtualized` StackedList, Feed or GridList is now a keyboard stop the list manages, on the web, in every browser. Each scrolls its rows (or tiles) in a list of its own, and none of them said whether that list was a tab stop. With read-only rows, Chromium and Firefox made it a tab stop of their own, with the browser's ring drawn on that inner list (blue in Chromium, black in Firefox; inside a StackedList's or Feed's card, and cut off at the edges of the avatar Feed's flush card), while WebKit left it out of the tab order, so Safari's keyboard could not reach the rows below the fold. The list is now a tab stop while its rows overflow and none while they fit, and while it has keyboard focus the theme's focus ring is drawn around the StackedList's or Feed's card (or a plain StackedList's frame) and around a GridList, which has no card of its own, as it is for a windowed DataTable's body. The rule holds whatever the rows hold, so a windowed list whose rows are buttons (a `clickable` StackedList, a Feed with `onItemPress`, a GridList with `onPressItem`) gains the list as one more stop, just before its first row, and the keyboard can always scroll it. The stop is an unnamed group: as a plain scroller, Chromium named it with the text of every row it rendered, so a screen reader would have read them all out on focus. A click into the rows draws no ring until a key is pressed. A list that renders eagerly (without `virtualized`, without a bounded height, or a `reorderable` StackedList) is unchanged.
